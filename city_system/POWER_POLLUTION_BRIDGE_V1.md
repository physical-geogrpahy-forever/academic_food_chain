# Power Emission -> Local Pollution Bridge V1

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: LOCKED FOR PRE-AUTOPLAY

## 1. Why this bridge exists

`POWER_SYSTEM_V1.md` intentionally stopped at emission classes because the project had not yet defined how a Gathering Storm fuel source should affect the separate Health system.

Health V1 later defined:

`Health pollution penalty = -floor(city_pollution / 10)`, capped at -5.

Therefore a numeric translation from Power generation to local city Pollution is now required before Health-Power runtime testing.

## 2. Gathering Storm source coefficients

Gathering Storm resource data uses:

- Coal: PowerProvided 4, CO2perkWh 820
- Oil: PowerProvided 4, CO2perkWh 490
- Uranium: PowerProvided 16, CO2perkWh 48

The project preserves these relative coefficients.

Global CO2 generated by a fuel source per turn is therefore:

`GlobalCO2 = ActualPowerGenerated * CO2perkWh`

The project's reserved-capacity fuel model does not permanently consume the resource, but it can still calculate emissions from actual Power output each turn.

## 3. Local Pollution normalization

Global atmospheric CO2 and local city-health Pollution are not the same variable.

For Health coupling, define the source city's local power Pollution load as:

`LocalPowerPollution = round(ActualPowerGenerated * CO2perkWh / 300)`

The divisor 300 is a project balance normalization, not a Gathering Storm source value.

It was selected so that under the already locked Health rule `10 Pollution = -1 Health`:

- a Coal plant supplying roughly one 4-Power resource-capacity block produces about 11 local Pollution and therefore -1 Health;
- an Oil plant at 4 Power produces about 7 local Pollution and does not cross -1 Health until it supplies roughly 8 Power;
- a Nuclear plant supplying 16 Power produces only about 3 local Pollution and therefore no direct Health penalty.

This keeps the source ordering and approximate relative cleanliness without making a single low-output fossil plant automatically max out the Health penalty.

## 4. Examples

### Coal

- 2 Power -> local Pollution 5 -> Health 0
- 4 Power -> 11 -> Health -1
- 8 Power -> 22 -> Health -2
- 12 Power -> 33 -> Health -3
- 16 Power -> 44 -> Health -4
- 24+ Power -> Health penalty reaches the existing -5 cap

### Oil

- 4 Power -> local Pollution 7 -> Health 0
- 8 Power -> 13 -> Health -1
- 12 Power -> 20 -> Health -2
- 24 Power -> 39 -> Health -3

### Uranium

- 16 Power -> local Pollution 3 -> Health 0
- 32 Power -> local Pollution 5 -> Health 0

Nuclear accident risk remains a separate system and is not represented as continuous Health loss.

## 5. Spatial attribution

Local Power Pollution belongs to the city containing the generating plant.

A networked recipient city receives Power but does not inherit the source city's local air-pollution Health penalty.

Global CO2, however, is added to the shared atmospheric total regardless of which city consumes the electricity.

This distinction prevents a single Coal plant from applying the same local Health penalty to every city in its six-hex Power network.

## 6. Renewables

The following adopted sources add 0 local Power Pollution and 0 fuel-based global CO2 in this gameplay layer:

- Solar Plant
- Solar Farm
- Wind Farm
- Offshore Wind Farm
- Geothermal Plant
- Hydroelectric Dam

Lifecycle emissions are outside the adopted Gathering Storm gameplay abstraction and are not added here.

## 7. Interaction with worked resources

Health V1 separately gives a worked Coal or Oil tile -1 Health under the original Health & Plague resource-yield concept.

This is not the same mechanism as Power Pollution:

- worked-resource Health is an extraction-site/local land-use penalty;
- Power Pollution is a consumption/generation externality in the plant city.

Both may apply, but autoplay must watch whether the combined burden is excessive.

## 8. Other industrial Pollution

This bridge covers only Power generation.

Any future numeric Pollution from Factory, Steelworks, transport, deforestation, units or other systems must enter `city_pollution` through their own explicit tables. Do not silently assume those values from this bridge.

## 9. Runtime watches

1. Coal-heavy cities supplying large regional networks may hit the -5 Health pollution cap quickly.
2. Oil may be too clean locally if typical network demand rarely exceeds 4 Power.
3. Nuclear may have effectively zero continuous Health cost, so its separate reactor-age/accident system becomes important for tradeoff.
4. Recycling Center's `CITY_POLLUTION_PERCENT=-25` must apply to local Pollution after all local sources are summed, not separately to each source.
5. Global CO2 pacing must be tested separately from local Health pacing.

## Final verdict

The previously deferred `Power emission class -> numeric local Pollution` bridge is now numerically defined for pre-autoplay testing.
