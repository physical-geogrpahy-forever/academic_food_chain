## Rule

Era assignment follows the person's **principal philosophical or intellectual activity**.

Do not classify by:
- birth year;
- death year;
- Nobel/prize year;
- a technology prerequisite chosen for gameplay;
- the publication date of one unrepresentative late work.

Use the period in which the candidate's representative philosophical program was formed, published and actively developed.

Locked boundaries:
- Ancient: before 500 BCE
- Classical: 500 BCE–299 CE
- Late Antiquity: 300–599
- Early Medieval: 600–999
- High Medieval: 1000–1299
- Renaissance: 1300–1499
- Exploration: 1500–1699
- Enlightenment: 1700–1799
- Industrial: 1800–1899
- Modern: 1900–1944
- Atomic: 1945–1989
- Information: 1990–present
- Future: no historical-person roster

## Relocations

### Laozi
**Ancient -> Classical, with historicity review retained**

Modern scholarship does not securely establish a single historical Laozi. The received Laozi/Daodejing appears to have developed through collections that expanded and consolidated especially during the fourth century BCE, reaching a relatively stable form by the third century BCE. If the named Laozi candidate is retained for gameplay, Classical is therefore a better textual-intellectual placement than Ancient.

### William James
**Industrial -> Modern**

James published important work before 1900, but his most important mature philosophical contributions cluster in the early twentieth century: Varieties of Religious Experience (1902), radical empiricism essays (1904–05), Pragmatism (1907) and A Pluralistic Universe (1909). His principal philosophical activity therefore falls on the Modern side of the 1900 boundary.

### Henri Bergson
**Industrial -> Modern**

Time and Free Will (1889) and Matter and Memory (1896) begin Bergson's career, but his major international philosophical influence and mature system center on 1900–1910, especially Introduction to Metaphysics (1903) and Creative Evolution (1907). Modern is the better principal-activity placement.

### Karl Popper
**Atomic -> Modern**

Popper's central philosophy-of-science program was already formulated in Logik der Forschung (1934). The Poverty of Historicism was written in the wartime period and his political philosophy crystallized around 1944–45. Because the core critical-rationalist and falsificationist program was formed before the 1945 boundary, Modern is used, with 1945 treated as a boundary continuation rather than the start of his philosophical identity.

### Jean-Paul Sartre
**Atomic -> Modern**

Being and Nothingness (1943) is the defining treatise of Sartrean existentialism. Sartre continued to develop existentialist Marxism after 1945, but his representative philosophical identity was already fully established before the Atomic boundary. Modern is therefore preferred.

## Boundary cases retained

### B. R. Ambedkar — Modern
Annihilation of Caste was first published in 1936 and is a representative philosophical and political text. Later constitutional work does not require moving his principal intellectual identity past 1945.

### Maurice Merleau-Ponty — Atomic
Phenomenology of Perception was completed in 1944 and published/defended in 1945. The project's boundary rule places this representative work at the opening of Atomic, and his mature postwar career continued afterward.

### John Rawls — Atomic
A Theory of Justice (1971) established justice as fairness in systematic form. Later restatements in 1993–2001 do not shift his principal philosophical activity out of Atomic.

### Jürgen Habermas — Atomic
His major philosophical formation spans the postwar decades, including The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere (1962) and The Theory of Communicative Action (1981). Later work continues the program rather than initiating it.

### Charles Taylor — Information
Taylor is a genuine 1989/1990 boundary case. Sources of the Self (1989) is immediately followed by major 1990s work on recognition, multiculturalism and secular modernity. For gameplay periodization, his mature representative activity is assigned to Information.

### Bruno Latour — Information
Latour's science-studies program begins before 1990, but his mature philosophical identity spans the 1990s and later, including We Have Never Been Modern (1991). Information is retained.

### Judith Butler — Information
Gender Trouble appeared in 1990 exactly at the Information-era threshold and established the representative philosophical program used for the candidate.

## Important non-moves

- Charles Sanders Peirce remains Industrial because the pragmatic maxim and key Illustrations of the Logic of Science papers date to 1877–78.
- Friedrich Nietzsche remains Industrial because his representative philosophical corpus is concentrated in the 1870s and 1880s.
- Gottlob Frege remains Industrial because Begriffsschrift (1879), Foundations of Arithmetic (1884) and the core sense/reference work of 1892–93 precede 1900.
- Bertrand Russell remains Modern because his foundational logic and analytic-philosophy work is concentrated in the early twentieth century.
- Ludwig Wittgenstein remains Modern because the Tractatus (1921) and the bulk of his philosophical formation occur before 1945, even though Philosophical Investigations was published posthumously in 1953.
- Hannah Arendt remains Atomic because her best-known and most influential philosophical works begin with The Origins of Totalitarianism (1951) and The Human Condition (1958).
- Simone de Beauvoir remains Atomic because The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947) and The Second Sex (1949) establish her mature philosophical program.
