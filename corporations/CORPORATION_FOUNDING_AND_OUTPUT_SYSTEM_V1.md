# Corporation Founding and Output System V1

Date: 2026-09-20
Status: LOCKED CORE DESIGN / FOUNDER ROSTER STILL EXPANDABLE

## 1. Core philosophy

Corporations are **not World Wonders**.

- sectors are repeatable and competitive;
- any civilization can establish a corporation in an eligible sector;
- a named historical firm may be unique, but its sector never becomes unavailable to others;
- corporations convert resources, technology, capital and networks into products/services;
- corporations can create new manufactured luxury goods, functional industrial goods, or services.

## 2. Great People who may found corporations

Two Great Person classes may found corporations:

### Great Merchant
Best suited to:
- finance
- trade
- retail
- food and consumer goods
- clothing/fashion
- cosmetics
- hospitality
- media distribution
- commercial services

Generic Great Merchant action:
**Found Corporation**
- consume Great Merchant;
- select any eligible Merchant-sector corporation;
- creates headquarters in the city;
- generated company name may be culture-appropriate if the Great Merchant is not a signature founder.

### Great Engineer
Best suited to:
- automotive
- machinery
- electrical engineering
- industrial materials
- chemicals
- electronics
- telecommunications
- precision engineering
- industrial energy
- advanced manufacturing

Generic Great Engineer action:
**Found Industrial Corporation**
- consume Great Engineer;
- select any eligible Engineer-sector corporation;
- creates headquarters in the city;
- requires the appropriate sector technology.

### Signature founder rule

A historically appropriate Great Merchant or Great Engineer may have a predetermined named company.

Examples:
- Thomas Edison -> Edison/General Electric lineage
- Carl Benz -> Benz & Cie.
- Henry Ford -> Ford Motor Company
- Robert Bosch -> Bosch
- Levi Strauss -> Levi Strauss & Co.
- Estée Lauder -> Estée Lauder
- Milton Hershey -> Hershey
- Fritz Hoffmann-La Roche -> Roche

A signature founder may still use the person's ordinary Great Person ability instead of founding the company.

The company is unique; the sector is not.

## 3. Founder-class assignment rule

Do not classify founders only by whether they became rich.

Use the historical basis of their contribution:

### Great Engineer founder
Use when the person's primary significance is:
- invention;
- engineering design;
- industrial process;
- technical manufacturing system;
- creation of a technology-intensive firm.

Strong examples:
- Carl Benz
- Thomas Edison
- Robert Bosch
- potentially Henry Ford, depending on the final Great Person roster

### Great Merchant founder
Use when the person's primary significance is:
- commerce;
- finance;
- retail;
- brand-building;
- distribution;
- entrepreneurship in consumer markets.

Strong examples:
- Levi Strauss
- Estée Lauder
- Milton Hershey
- Sam Walton
- Conrad Hilton

Borderline founders are evaluated individually rather than forced into one class by sector.

## 4. Three corporate-output classes

### A. Manufactured Luxury

A new consumer product category created by industry rather than found naturally on the map.

Examples:
- Jeans
- Cosmetics
- Chocolate
- Furniture
- Automobiles
- Consumer Electronics
- Watches
- Fashion Goods
- Soft Drinks

Main effects:
- Amenities / Happiness
- Gold from consumption and exports
- possible Culture/Tourism for globally iconic products

Important:
multiple brands in the same category do NOT count as fully separate luxury categories.

Example:
Ford, Toyota and Hyundai may all produce Automobiles.
The first Automobile brand gives the main Amenity effect.
Additional automobile brands retain Gold, trade, branding and Tourism value with reduced or no additional Amenity stacking.

## 5. Functional Corporate Goods

These are produced inputs used by cities, infrastructure or military/economic systems.

They are not generic happiness luxuries.

| Functional good | Inputs | Primary bonuses |
|---|---|---|
| Refined Petroleum Products | Oil | Production, transport/logistics, modern infrastructure |
| Steel Products | Iron/Coal | Production, construction |
| Machine Tools | Steel + industrial capacity | Production, Factory/Workshop efficiency |
| Fertilizer | chemical inputs | Food, Farm productivity |
| Pharmaceuticals | Chemistry/Biology inputs | Growth, Health, unit recovery |
| Electrical Equipment | Copper/Aluminum + Electricity | Production, Science |
| Precision Components | Steel/Electronics | Production, advanced-unit construction |
| Semiconductors | advanced electronics/materials | Science, Production, advanced units |
| Construction Materials | Stone/Steel/etc. | Building and infrastructure production |
| Processed Food | agricultural resources | Food storage, Growth, trade |
| Industrial Chemicals | Oil/Salt/chemical feedstocks | Production, advanced industry |
| Paper Products | Timber/pulp | Culture, education, administration |

### Refined petroleum example

Oil -> Petroleum/Refining corporation -> Refined Petroleum Products

Supplied city can receive:
- Production bonus;
- faster or cheaper transport/logistics;
- bonus to industrial and transportation infrastructure;
- reduced economic burden of oil-consuming civilian/modern systems.

Raw Oil remains the strategic resource.
Refined Petroleum Products are the value-added corporate output.

## 6. Functional goods must have supply

Avoid a single headquarters giving a permanent empire-wide modifier.

Preferred model:
- headquarters and corporate factories produce quantities;
- cities consume quantities;
- output can be allocated domestically or exported;
- unsupplied cities do not receive the bonus;
- larger empires therefore require larger production and distribution networks.

This gives corporations actual production and logistics gameplay.

## 7. Services

A service is usually not represented as a physical resource token.

A city receives a service through:
- local corporate branch;
- trade-route connection;
- national network;
- or contract/access mechanism.

Services are sector-specific.

| Service sector | Primary effects |
|---|---|
| Finance / Banking | Gold, investment, corporation expansion |
| Retail / Distribution | Gold, internal trade, manufactured-luxury circulation |
| Hospitality / Hotels | Tourism, Culture, Gold |
| Film / Entertainment | Culture, Tourism, Great Director points / film distribution |
| Media / Publishing | Culture, Tourism, ideological/civic spread |
| Telecommunications | Science, Gold, communication/trade efficiency |
| Logistics / Shipping | trade-route capacity, movement/logistics, Gold |
| Insurance | reduced disaster/business losses, Gold/economic stability |
| Advertising / Marketing | corporate product demand, Gold, Tourism |
| Professional Services | Production, Science, Gold depending on sector |
| Software / Digital Services | Science, Production, Gold |
| Airlines / Travel | Tourism, trade connectivity, movement |

Therefore **services do not all give Culture/Tourism**.

Culture/Tourism is especially appropriate for:
- hotels;
- film;
- media;
- travel;
- advertising/brands.

Finance and logistics instead affect Gold, investment and trade.
Telecommunications/software can affect Science and Productivity.

## 8. Corporate branch model

Each corporation has:

- founder Great Person
- headquarters city
- sector
- required civic
- required technology
- required resource/input(s)
- output class
- product/service
- branch offices
- production capacity
- domestic demand
- foreign demand
- corporate profit
- branding level
- foreign market presence

Branch offices are ordinary economic infrastructure, not Wonders.

A foreign branch normally needs:
- Open Borders / trade access / diplomatic-economic permission;
- a trade route or network connection;
- sufficient market demand.

## 9. Great Merchant / Great Engineer differentiation

### Great Merchant corporation
Typical advantage:
- cheaper branch expansion;
- stronger Gold;
- stronger demand/export;
- better foreign-market penetration.

### Great Engineer corporation
Typical advantage:
- stronger functional-good output;
- higher Production;
- technology transfer / Science;
- more efficient factories;
- lower strategic-resource input cost.

Signature founders may override these generic tendencies.

## 10. Interaction with civics and technology

- plus sector-specific technology

Examples:
- apparel corporation: Capitalism + Mass Production

- pharmaceutical corporation: Capitalism + Chemistry/Biology

- telecommunications corporation: Capitalism + Telecommunications

Do not create a separate Corporations civic unless implementation later exposes a real gap.
